Understanding Autism (ASD)

Before proceeding with the screening, it's vital to establish a medically accurate foundation. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition, not a disease. It cannot be "cured," and it does not need to be. It is a natural variation in the human neurological blueprint.

The Medical Reality

According to global medical consensus, ASD affects how people interact with others, communicate, learn, and behave. The word "spectrum" highlights the wide variation in challenges and strengths possessed by each autistic person. Some may require significant daily support, while others live entirely independently, often leveraging their hyper-focus and pattern-recognition skills in highly specialized fields.

Key Characteristics

  • Social Communication: Differences in processing social cues, maintaining eye contact, or understanding non-verbal communication.
  • Repetitive Behaviors: Engaging in routines or repetitive movements (stimming) to self-regulate sensory input.
  • Sensory Processing: Hyper- or hypo-reactivity to sensory stimuli like lights, sounds, and textures.
  • Deep Focus: Highly intense, passionate interests in specific topics.
